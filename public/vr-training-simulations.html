Example concept: follow a guided scenario with clear decision points.

Turn a procedure into an experience.

VR training places a learner inside a simulated situation. An experience can introduce equipment, guide a sequence of actions, or present a decision that changes what happens next. The scenario is designed around a learning objective and reviewed with the people who understand the real procedure.

  1. Set the objective. Choose a task or decision to practise, define the scenario boundaries, and agree what successful completion means.
  2. Practise in context. Use prompts, equipment interactions, and checkpoints to guide a learner through the virtual task.
  3. Reflect and repeat. Review actions and missed steps, discuss the reasoning, and repeat the scenario with a different level of guidance.

Illustrative use case

A guided equipment inspection

Imagine a new team member learning an inspection sequence. The simulation introduces the workspace, highlights checkpoints, and asks the learner to identify a simulated fault. A debrief explains which steps were completed and which need another attempt.

The aim: support repeatable practice and useful feedback. The example is illustrative, not a validated safety course or substitute for required practical training.
